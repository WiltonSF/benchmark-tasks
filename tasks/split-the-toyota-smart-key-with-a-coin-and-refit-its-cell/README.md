# Split a Toyota smart key with a screwdriver

Drivers open a depleted smart key and replace its CR2032. This partly measured HYQ14FBA-referenced stand-in requires the left hand to hold the two-half shell while the right twists a flat screwdriver in the seam, removes the fully detached cover, exchanges the cell, and presses both snaps shut. The final released state decides the score.

## Contact mechanism and scoring

Two separate rigid halves have native D6 joints with translational spring drives at x=-30.3/+30.3 mm in the case frame. Each drive holds the engaged snap and applies equal reaction to both halves. Opening disables a snap only during actual tool/hand contact after the stated load/travel history; the freed cover has no joint. Re-engagement occurs only after a robot press has already aligned and closed that end to <=0.5 mm lateral distance and <=0.5 mm absolute normal offset. Once re-enabled, each snap has normal stiffness 8,333 N/m (10 N cap): at a 0.5 mm normal offset its spring component is about 4.17 N before damping. Its lateral locating drives have stiffness 20,000 N/m (40 N caps), giving up to 10 N spring force at 0.5 mm lateral offset. These forces react equally on both halves and can finish seating an already pressed, aligned end. No pose write or capture window places a separated cover.

Each opening snap resists 10 N over 1.2 mm estimated travel. The notch snap releases only after that travel, at least 9.21 N paired blade jaw load on the actual flat-tip volume (shaft contact excluded), right/tool contact, left/base contact and lowest case point above both 50 mm over the table and 5 mm above the tallest passive support. The far snap requires travel under a right-hand pull against the held base. Native drive stiffness 8,333 N/m and damping 2 N s/m are estimates. A closing press must reach 12 N total and at least 6 N load share at each snap it engages, with that end at <=0.5 mm lateral distance and <=0.5 mm absolute normal offset. The final scoring gap separately limits each corner’s 3D displacement to <=0.5 mm. Force share follows the lever rule about the opposite snap.

The previous 0.12 N m coin torque / 2 degree turn / 1.984 mJ work and 0.72 N m hinge-closing moment described a different coin/hinge mechanism. The revision uses separate snap displacement/load gates because a fully detached cover has no hinge moment. This model change follows the cover-separation correction; it was not selected from a failed demo score. Opening torque/work remain recorded as diagnostics. The 10 N opening snap remains an estimate. Five measured centre closing presses were 711/864/923/843/799 g (mean 828 g = 8.1 N, range 7.0-9.1 N); the 12 N gate is retained above that range, rather than lowered. No physical equivalence is asserted. No spring fetches or places a separated cover.

Score 0 after a mat exit, unknown/stale state, absent loaded split, cover never fully detached or absent final hands-clear/stillness. Then 0.25 split; 0.5 also old cell removed and green replacement seated plus up; 0.75 also both ends snapped flush; 1 also old cell in tray. Wrong polarity caps at 0.25. No earlier best score is retained. Seating tolerance 2 mm radial / 1 mm height / 12 degrees face normal; maximum seam gap 0.5 mm. Old-cell removal needs a witnessed 30 mm displacement and final 11 mm clearance outside the base envelope. All parts must move <=1 mm / 1 degree in a 1.1 s window, speeds below 3 mm/s and 0.05 rad/s, followed by 1 s continuous hands-clear/stillness; hands clear each part by 5 mm.

**Closed-case inspection:** after the final photo, the operator reopens the case with the same tool and takes a top photograph and two orthogonal side photographs with a ruler: green replacement + up and centre within 2 mm of the seat (also check 1 mm height and 12 degree face tolerance). The closed-case photo establishes the seam before inspection disturbs it. A working-key button/LED recording is optional and does not alter the score. Video establishes the opening and exchange history; native telemetry establishes simulated force/work. Instrumented stock-shell measurements are necessary to compare real force history.

Near-miss cases use fabricated states to test scoring only: set-on unsnapped cover, minus-up cell under a closed cover, never-detached cover, one snap engaged, stale/missing state, short release, and mat violation. Six additional synthetic controls exercise the actual release callback: supported key, valid free-air load, absent left/right contact, single-jaw load and short travel. These tests are not physical demos.

## Distinctness

PR 2410/TK-3RDX7X (clock battery swap), PR 1838/TK-CDVB8C (server CR2032) and PR 1165/TK-DXM2J8 (smoke alarm) cover related cell handling. [Merged PR 2816](https://github.com/robocurve/benchmark-tasks/pull/2816), “Secure an open coin-cell remote before returning it to the toy shelf,” starts with an open remote and addresses safe placement. PR 3193, “Fold a car's flip key shut in the hands,” is closed/final and concerns a release button and hinged blade. The proposed additional skill here is loaded seam splitting of a closed, light two-half shell held against the other hand; the cell swap alone earns 0. The halves fully separate, and the other hand must resist the seam-pry reaction. That difference does not guarantee a distinctness verdict.
