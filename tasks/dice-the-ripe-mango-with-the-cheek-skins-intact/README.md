# Hold a mango cheek steady, score it into a grid down to but not through the skin, then push the skin up to fan the cubes

**Instruction:** Steady the mango cheek, score its flesh into a crosshatch grid of at least 10 cubes down to the skin
with the paring knife without cutting through the skin, press the skin side onto the upturned ramekin so the cubes
fan out, then leave the cheek, the knife and the ramekin on the board and let go.

One cheek of a ripe mango, already cut off the stone, lies skin side down on a cutting board. The left gripper pinches
its narrow near end to keep it from sliding. The right gripper picks up a paring knife by its handle and, holding it
point down, draws seven cuts through the flesh, four across the cheek and three along it, each running down to just
above the skin, which curves up from 24 mm deep at the middle to nothing at the edge. It stands the knife back on its
handle and lets go; the left gripper lets go of the cheek. The right gripper then picks the cheek up by both sides of
its rim and presses the middle of the skin onto the base of an upturned ramekin, against the skin's stated resistance
to bending, until the skin's middle is pushed up 28 mm or more, past flat, so the cup turns out into a dome and the
scored cubes fan apart (the "hedgehog" cut), sets the cheek back on the board and lets go.

## Changes in this revision

- **Skin-bending resistance.** Pressing the cheek onto the ramekin now meets a declared resistance (0.2 N per mm up to
  5 N, with an equal reaction on the ramekin), so the holding gripper has to push the skin up against it (basis and a
  kitchen-scale check below).
- **Full credit needs the cubes fanned.** The skin's middle must be pushed up 28 mm, 2.5 mm past flat, so the cup is
  turned out into a dome, against the full 5 N (before: 12 mm, with no resistance).
- **Split check.** Push, split and resistance are read only for a ramekin whose base presses on the skin from below,
  with its centre inside the cheek's outline. A ramekin on the cut-face side of the cheek, brought down from that side
  or slid in through the cheek's side (more than about 5 mm above the skin's lowest point) cannot start a press, a
  split or the resistance.
- **Blade face.** The skin test samples both faces of the blade in eight rows from the edge up to the spine, not only
  the edge and spine.
- **Real-trial checks.** The push is read as the cheek's rim coming 2.5 mm or more below the ramekin's base with the
  base under the middle of the skin; the push force is checked on a kitchen scale once per session; gripper covers,
  sleeves and towel checks keep juice off the rig, checked by a practice trial before each session's trials.
- The scripted demo and Dream-1 were rerun on this version (Results).

## Why this task

- It is the core of the standard way to cube a mango. The National Mango Board's steps: cut the cheeks off beside the
  seed, then "Score (slice) the flesh in a crosshatch pattern, without cutting through the skin", then push the
  centre of the skin upward to fan the cubes outward
  ([mango.org, How to Cut a Mango](https://www.mango.org/how-to-cut-a-mango/)).
  The first step (cutting the cheek off the stone) is done by the assessor at reset; this task is the scoring and
  turning out of one cheek, which fits the 120 s limit.
- The hard part is depth along a hidden, curved boundary. The cut face is flat but the skin under it is a cup: the
  flesh is 24 mm thick in the middle and thins to nothing at the edge, so a knife held at one height either stops
  short in the middle (the cubes stay joined and do not fan out) or goes through the skin at the edge. The point has
  to follow the cup down and up again on every cut, against a stated cutting drag; at cutting speed (above about
  6 mm/s) the drag is more than the cheek's board friction, and at any speed a cut counts only while the other hand
  pinches the cheek.

## Score

**Score 0 for the trial, whatever happens later, if at any moment:**
- the knife cuts through the skin: any part of the blade (its edge, either face or its spine) goes more than 1 mm
  into the 1.5 mm skin anywhere more than 2 mm in from the cheek's edge (within 2 mm of the edge the skin turns up to
  meet the cut face and is not judged);
- the blade touches either gripper, or comes within 10 mm of a gripper that is not holding the knife by its handle;
- while a gripper holds the cheek, the base of the ramekin, pressing up into the skin from below, is pushed more than
  35 mm into the skin from its resting shape, 9.5 mm past flat (an over-push, the simulation's stand-in for split
  skin). A ramekin on the cut-face side of the cheek (above a cheek lying skin side down), one brought down onto the
  cheek from that side (even through a lifted cheek), one slid in through the cheek's side more than about 5 mm above
  the skin's lowest point, or a cheek turned cut face down over it never counts as a push or a split, and meets no resistance.

**Otherwise it is judged at the end: both grippers at least 5 mm from the cheek, the knife and the ramekin; the
cheek skin side down, the knife and the ramekin resting on the board; no part of the blade in or within 2 mm of the
cheek; nothing moving more than 1 mm or 2 degrees for 1 s. If these end conditions are not met, the score is 0.**

A **cube** is a piece of the cut face that cuts reaching within 6 mm of the skin separate from every other piece
(flesh within 7 mm of the cheek's edge is rim and belongs to no piece), with a top of 80 to 800 mm2 and no two
points of its top more than 30 mm apart.

Only the highest level reached counts. Lots A and B are averaged.

A gripper pinches the cheek when its opening is 10 to 90 mm and both fingertips are within 3 mm of the cheek with their
contact points at least 15 mm apart across the cut face (the rule checks position, not force). A cut counts only
while the knife is held by its handle in one gripper, the cheek is pinched by the other, the blade
is within 45 degrees of upright, and the edge moves within 45 degrees of the blade's own plane (drawn or pushed point
first, not dragged sideways), with the cutting drag acting (below). Cuts made after the push do not count.

**How the simulation scores it (`task.py`).** `update()` runs every control step. The cut face is a grid of 1 mm
cells; for each cell it keeps the lowest point the edge of the knife reached there during counted cuts, sampled
every 0.5 mm of edge motion relative to the cheek and every 1 mm along the edge. A cell is cut when that point is
within 6 mm of the skin. Cubes are the 4-connected groups of uncut cells inside the 7 mm rim band. The skin test
uses the whole blade, whether or not a cut counts: the edge, and both faces of the 2 mm thick blade (1 mm either
side of the edge's plane), each sampled in eight rows from just above the edge up to the spine (2.25 mm apart at the
heel, 1 mm in the point section) and every 1 mm along the blade, at the same 0.5 mm motion sampling. The
ramekin's flat base is sampled at its centre and on rings of 8, 16 and 24 mm radius (its edge), 45 points, every
control step. A base point "touches" when it is inside the cheek's outline and above its undisturbed outer skin, at
any height. Push, split and the skin-bending resistance below are read only while the ramekin stands base up
relative to the cheek (axes within 30 degrees) and its base is pressing into the skin from below: from a step on
which the base touches with its centre inside the cheek's outline and below the cut face, having not touched on the
step before, for as long as the base keeps touching with its centre inside the outline. A ramekin on the cut-face
side of the cheek already touches before it reaches the cut face, and one slid in through the cheek's side touches
with its edge before its centre is inside the outline, so neither can start a press or a split (unless it slides in
within about 5 mm of the skin's lowest point, where the skin is nearly flat and the contact counts as a press from
below). The push is the
height of the ramekin's flat base above the cheek's undisturbed outer skin surface at the base's centre, which must
be within 15 mm of the cheek's centre, with the ramekin base up; past the cup's 25.5 mm depth the skin's middle
stands above its rim (turned out). Full credit needs a push of 28 mm or more while the cheek is upright (within 30
degrees) and pinched, with the full 5 N of skin-bending resistance acting on the step before (so a jab that
outruns the resistance does not count); 35 mm at any base point, while the cheek is pinched, is the split limit.
Hand distances use the grippers' collision-hull faces sampled every 3 mm. `score()` returns the level; `success()`
is score 1.0.

## Cutting drag (declared material response)

While any part of the edge is in the flesh (until the push), the simulation applies a drag to the knife along the blade's horizontal
direction, against the edge's motion along it, and a feed resistance upward while the edge moves down. Each grows
with the edge's speed relative to the cheek (its motion over the last 0.1 s) and reaches 1 N at 10 mm/s (the demo
cuts at 4-14 mm/s); the equal and opposite force acts on the cheek. Both act at the bodies' centres of mass; the moment
about the knife grip is not simulated. A cut counts only on steps when this load was acting, with the edge, averaged
over the last 0.25 s, moving at 1 mm/s or more. (A load taken from the 0.25 s average lagged the knife's motion and
set it bouncing about 5 mm up and down during cuts across, so the load uses the shorter 0.1 s window.)
The values are estimates for a sharp paring knife in ripe mango flesh: firmness probes with an 8 mm plunger record roughly 5-15 N for ripe mango (a pressure of
0.1-0.3 MPa), and a thin edge with about 2 mm2 of edge in the flesh needs well under 1 N to part it, plus some side
friction. Above about 6 mm/s the drag is more than the unheld 115 g cheek's board friction (about 0.6 N), so an
unheld cheek slides; at any speed a cut counts only while the other hand pinches the cheek.

## Skin-bending resistance (declared material response)

While the ramekin's flat base presses into the skin from below (as defined under Score), the simulation pushes the
cheek away from the base along the cheek's own up axis, and pushes the ramekin back the other way, with a force of
0.2 N for every millimetre the deepest point of the base has gone past the undisturbed outer skin, up to 5 N (reached
at 25 mm, about where the skin is flat at 25.5 mm, and held at 5 N while it is turned further out). The force rises
by at most 0.5 N per control step, falls at once with the press, and is zero whenever the base is not pressing.
Both forces act at the bodies' centres of mass. To turn the skin out to the 28 mm full-credit push, the gripper
holding the cheek therefore has to press down with about 3.9 N on top of the cheek's own 1.1 N weight, and its
friction on the rim has to carry that. The cheek does not change shape on screen.

Basis (an estimate): the point-load stiffness of a thin, shallow spherical shell, k = 4Et^2/(R sqrt(3(1-v^2))), with
peel thickness t = 1.5 mm, Poisson's ratio v about 0.45, an assumed modulus E about 1.5 MPa for ripe, softened peel
and the modelled cheek's radius of curvature, R = 38 mm across it and 74 mm along it (a sphere through its rim and
the middle of its skin), gives 0.12 to 0.23 N/mm; 0.2 N/mm is used. Flesh above the cut bottoms parts at the cuts;
the uncut layer under them (up to 6 mm) adds stiffness that is not included. A shell pushed far softens as it snaps
through, hence the cap at 5 N (about 510 g on a kitchen scale, a light press). No mango was measured for this; the
real trial measures it (below).

## Real trial

**Fruit.** One Ataulfo (Honey) mango per trial, 280 to 350 g, ripe: a 10 N press with a 20 mm flat-ended probe on
its shoulder (fruit on a kitchen scale reading 1 kg) dents it 2 to 4 mm, read with a ruler; no soft spots, bruises or
cracks. Ripen and keep it at room temperature (18 to 24 C), not refrigerated, and use it the day it passes the press
check. A trial takes about 12 minutes of assessor time: about 2 to cut and check the cheek, 5 for the checks after
the run and 5 to clean the rig and refit the covers.

**Cutting the cheek (assessor, at reset, about 2 minutes).** Stand the mango on its stem end, narrow side toward
you. With a chef's knife, cut down about 6 mm to one side of the centre line, just clearing the flat seed, as in
the mango.org steps. Keep that cheek if its cut face is 105 to 120 mm long and 66 to 78 mm wide, it is 23 to 28 mm
deep at the middle (ruler across the face, depth gauge or skewer to the skin) and the cut face is whole (no seed
fibres torn out deeper than 3 mm); otherwise cut the other side or use a new mango. Weigh it (1 g scale) and
photograph the cut face with the ruler. Lay it skin side down at the lot's place with its narrower end (the fruit's
tip, away from the stem) toward the robot's left arm (the modelled cheek is the same at both ends).

**Before the run.** Knife, ramekin and ruler as listed. Fit clean, food-safe, cut-resistant covers (EN 388 cut level
C or higher) over the gripper pads; their thickness, and that of the finger cots and cuff tape below, counts in the
grasp width. Two cameras: one at the side, level with the ramekin's base, with a 1 mm scale; one above the board.
Measure the cheek's depth at the middle (skewer marked in mm) before the run.

**Push resistance (once per session).** Score a spare cheek by hand, with the cuts stopping 3 to 6 mm above the
skin. Stand the upturned ramekin on a kitchen scale (1 g, tared), hold the cheek level by its rim and press the
middle of its skin onto the base, with a ruler standing beside the ramekin. Note the reading when the rim has come
down 10 mm from first touch (declared 2 N, about 200 g) and when the rim is 2.5 mm below the level of the base, the
full push (declared 5 N, about 510 g). Record both with the results; a reading less than half or more than twice the
declared value is reported as a mismatch with the declared estimate.

**Keeping juice off the rig.** Mango juice and pulp touch only the board, the knife, the ramekin and the gripper
covers; nothing from the fruit may reach the shared robot. On both grippers each cut-resistant cover encloses its
finger and pad completely, with a single-use food-grade nitrile finger cot pulled over it and taped at the cuff
(food-safe tape that peels off clean) so juice cannot run under it; a clear food-safe plastic sleeve is taped over
each gripper body and wrist down to the finger cuffs, with the wrist camera looking through clear film; a clear 0.1 mm
food-safe plastic sheet is taped to the bench under the board and 10 cm around it (the board's height is unchanged).
After each trial, with the robot stopped: peel off and bin the finger cots, remove the covers (washed in hot soapy
water and dried before reuse, or replaced), wipe each finger, sleeve, wrist and camera film with a damp cloth and then
a 70% isopropyl wipe, and rub a white paper towel over each finger and wrist under a bright light. Any yellow trace
or stickiness means cleaning again before the next trial. Replace the plastic sheet and wipe the bench under it the
same way.

**Confirming the covers work (before the first trial of each session, and after any change of covers, cots or
tape).** Run one complete practice trial, by teleoperation or with the policy, on a spare scored cheek, including
the press on the ramekin. Then, with the robot stopped, take off the cots, covers and sleeves and rub a fresh white
paper towel over each bare finger, pad, gripper body, wrist and camera window under a bright light. The covers pass
only if every towel stays clean (no yellow, no stickiness); otherwise fit new cots and more tape and repeat. Record
the result, with a photo of the towels, alongside the trial results. Trials start only after a pass.

**After the run (about 5 minutes).** Do not touch anything until the video has ended. Then:
1. Photograph the cheek from above with the ruler. If the skin was pushed up, the cubes stand apart with open gaps.
2. Count the cubes: pieces separated on every side by cuts (or by the 7 mm rim band), 80 to 800 mm2 on top and no
   more than 30 mm corner to corner (ruler).
3. Check depth: on each cut, at both ends and the middle, part the flesh gently with the knife tip, push the marked
   skewer to the bottom of the cut, then beside the cut down to the skin; the cut counts there if the two readings
   differ by 6 mm or less. Cuts count only if made while the other gripper held the cheek (top video).
4. Check the skin: turn the cheek over and hold it against a bright light; any slit through the skin more than
   2 mm in from the edge is a cut through (score 0). (The simulation's rule, more than 1 mm into the 1.5 mm skin, is
   a stricter stand-in for a visible slit.) Read how far the middle of the skin was pushed from the side video
   against the 1 mm scale: while the cheek is held level on the ramekin, with the base's centre within 15 mm of the
   middle of the skin (top video), its rim must come down to 2.5 mm or more below the level of the ramekin's base
   (from first touch of the skin's middle on the base, the cup's measured depth plus 2.5 mm), so the skin's middle
   stands above the rim and the cut face is domed; the top video records the cubes standing apart during that hold
   (not scored). A tear in the skin seen against the light scores 0 (the simulation's limit, more than 35 mm or 9.5 mm
   past flat at any point of the base, is its stand-in for a split; in a real trial record how far below the base the
   rim went). Step through the side and top videos frame by frame for the 10 mm gap between the blade and the
   steadying gripper, using whichever view shows it clearly.

**Reset.** A new mango and cheek; the board, knife and ramekin washed and dried; pad covers washed and dried or
replaced, new finger cots, sleeves wiped, and the rig cleaned and checked with white paper towels as above. All fruit
is thrown away.

**Safety.** The knife is a real sharp knife. Run real trials only with the rig's stop and guard procedures and
nobody within the arms' reach. The 10 mm blade rule protects the steadying gripper.

## What is simplified (fidelity)

- The cheek is one rigid 115 g body built from 58 stacked boxes in the shape of half an ellipsoid (drawing:
  `fixture/cheek.svg`). It does not bend, so the push is a declared rule: the ramekin passes into the rigid cheek
  (their collision is switched off) against the declared skin-bending resistance above, and 28 mm or more of overlap
  at the skin's middle counts as the skin pushed up that far: 2.5 mm past flat (at 25.5 mm the skin is flat), so the
  cup is turned out into a dome and the cuts open. The push is read only while the base presses from below (as under
  Score); past 35 mm anywhere on the base while the cheek is held (9.5 mm past flat) is an over-push, scored as split
  skin. On screen the ramekin's base passes into the cheek, the cheek does not change shape, and the cubes are not
  shown fanning out. Completed cuts are drawn as dark 4 mm squares on the cut
  face, shown as soon as the scorer records a cut reaching within 6 mm of the skin under them; they are a drawing
  only and play no part in scoring.
- PhysX cannot cut. The blade has no collider (the handle and guard do); scoring is the declared cell rule above,
  with the stated drag and feed forces so the knife hand has to push through and the other hand has to hold the
  cheek. The cubes are not separate bodies.
- The skin is 1.5 mm thick everywhere; real skin is 1 to 2 mm. Ripeness, flesh strength and the forces are estimates;
  no mango or knife was measured.
- The knife stands on its handle so a top-down gripper can take it; in a kitchen it would lie flat.

## Demo

The scripted demo (`demo()`) plays fixed waypoints worked out from each lot's published layout and the frozen
corrections below; it reads no object state during a trial.

- The left gripper leans 45 degrees toward the far end and pinches the cheek's narrow near end across its width, at
  the top 4 mm of its sides, 50 mm from the middle of the cut face.
- The right gripper takes the knife by its handle (15 mm behind the handle's centre), tips it 45 degrees point down
  and makes four cuts across the cheek (at 38, 24, 8 and -6 mm along it, `CROSS_X`) and three along it (at -16, 0
  and 16 mm, `LONG_Y`, from the far edge to 11 mm past the middle toward the held end). Each cut starts 3.5 mm inside
  the edge (the cuts across end 5 mm inside the edge where they finish, because the blade's upper front reaches ahead
  of the point) and moves point first, so the blade behind the point stays over
  deeper flesh: 14 mm/s, slowed to 4 mm/s over the last 12 mm of each cut across and to 7 mm/s over 8-25 mm and the
  last 10 mm of each cut along, where the point otherwise trails its waypoints. The point is aimed 3 mm above the
  skin (4 mm over the first 8 mm; over the last 12 mm, 2 mm on a cut across and 3.5 mm on a cut along), following the
  cup down and up again, and never sent more
  than 0.5 mm below the skin's inner surface at the commanded spot itself. At the end of each cut the knife lifts
  straight out. Between the two sets of cuts the knife is levelled and turned beside the board. After the last cut
  the point backs 50 mm away from the held end and the knife is carried off high.
- It stands the knife back on its handle where it started and lets go; the left gripper lets go of the cheek and
  moves away.
- The right gripper pinches both sides of the cheek's rim at the middle, lifts it 45 mm and lowers it onto the
  upturned ramekin, sending the skin's lowest point 42.5 mm below the ramekin's base (`PRESS`). The arm gives under
  the skin-bending resistance, so the skin's middle ends about 31 mm up (31.3 mm in lot A, 31.6 mm in lot B in a traced
  run of the same motion and resistance) against the full 5 N, inside the 28 to 35 mm window; the final runs scored
  1.0 in both lots, which needs a push of 28 mm or more. It holds 1.2 s, lifts the cheek off, sets it back where it was
  and lets go.

`CAL` in `task.py` holds fixed corrections for each lot and each set of cuts: the knife arm sits a little off, lags
behind along the cut and rides up under the stated cutting load, so the scoring waypoints lead along the cut by about
5 mm at full speed (scaled with speed, tapering to nothing over the last 12 mm) and aim a millimetre or so lower. In
both lots the last waypoint of each cut across is raised (3.5 mm in lot A, 2.5 mm in lot B, rising from the waypoint
before it): those cuts end close to the rim, and in traced runs the arm's stored lag sprang the point 2-3 mm on
into the rising skin when the stroke stopped. The corrections were measured from the task's own logs of traced runs and then frozen; the
demo never reads a live pose.

```sh
inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy yam_scripted \
  -E task=task.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 \
  --instruction "Steady the mango cheek, score its flesh into a crosshatch grid of at least 10 cubes down to the skin with the paring knife without cutting through the skin, press the skin side onto the upturned ramekin so the cubes fan out, then leave the cheek, the knife and the ramekin on the board and let go." \
  --scorer task_score --max-steps 2400 --epochs 2 --store-frames
```

`--epochs 2` runs lot A, then lot B; the score is their mean. `TASK_LOT=A` or `B` pins one lot. For Dream-1 use
`--policy dream`.

## How this differs from close tasks

- TK-V7P9X9 (slit a carton seam short of the product 6 mm below): one straight cut at one depth over a flat layer;
  here the depth limit is a hidden cup, so the point has to rise and fall along every cut, and seven cuts must close
  cubes on all sides.
- TK-N9C8DA (hold a loaf while sawing a slice) and TK-53WVPU (cut a held cord): cut through the whole object; here
  the cut must stop just short of the skin.
- TK-VT2J8N (peel a cucumber) and TK-SANNN9 (scratch off a coating): remove an outer layer; here the outer layer
  must stay whole while the flesh above it is divided.
- TK-JRVAY3 (grate cheese): no knife, no depth limit.
