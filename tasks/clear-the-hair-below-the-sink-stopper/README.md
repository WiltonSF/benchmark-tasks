# Clear the hair from under a sink's open pop-up stopper: feed the hooked wand in under its head and draw the hair out

A bathroom vanity with a Kohler Caxton under-mount basin and a Kohler K-7114 pop-up drain. The stopper stands open.
Below it, out of sight in the drain, a clump of hair is caught round the stopper's stem and pivot rod: the usual
cause of a slow bathroom sink. A FlexiSnake Drain Weasel PLUS hair-clog tool (reusable handle with its 12-inch
disposable wand) lies on the countertop.

Instruction: `Clear the hair from under the sink stopper, then leave the tool and the hair on the counter.`

The stopper's head is wider than the drain's throat, so from above it covers the drain. The thin, flexible wand has
to go in under the head's edge, bend down the bore beside the stopper's guide fins, reach the hair, and come back out
with it; the hair cannot be reached with the fingers, and the stopper cannot be lifted out (its pivot rod runs
through it). The wound hair holds on: the pad's barbs catch it only when drawn back through the clump, and it has to be
pulled steadily up the bore against its resistance. The maker's package insert: "Insert The Drain Weasel into sink, shower or tub drain opening. The Drain
Weasel will slide past most drain pop-up stoppers and grates." and "Carefully pull snake and debris from the drain."

Category: Societal relatability (a household chore most people with a bathroom sink face). O*NET 47-2152.00 (Plumbers,
Pipefitters, and Steamfitters) lists as a core task "Maintain or repair plumbing by replacing defective washers,
replacing or mending broken pipes, or opening clogged drains." This task is the household version of the last part.

## Scoring (from video, a top photo, a straightedge, a ruler and a scale)

**Score 0 if, at any moment:** the tool drops off the vanity (any part of it more than 20 mm below the countertop
outside the vanity's outline: on the floor or hanging off the top's edge).

**Otherwise, the end state.** Every tier needs both grippers at least 10 mm from the tool and the tool still for the
last 1 s (under 3 mm/s, drifting under 1 mm). "The hair" means at least 0.40 g of the 0.50 g clump, blotted and
air-dried for 1 h and then weighed on a 0.01 g scale after the trial (what is on the pad, on the counter or in the basin, and what is fished out of the
drain, are weighed separately). If the clump splits, each tier needs 0.40 g at the place it names: "out of the drain"
counts all hair weighed outside the drain; "at the countertop's height" counts only hair there. "At the countertop's
height" means inside the vanity's outline and no lower than 10 mm under the stone's surface: on the stone, or on the
pad where the wand's end reaches past the basin's rim. Only hair the tool drew out counts (in the simulation, only
hair snagged and pulled out against its load by the declared rule below). "The whole tool lies on the countertop"
means: both ends of its square sleeve on the stone; the handle reaching no more than 75 mm past the top's edge (it
starts with 68 mm (lot A) or 58 mm (lot B) past the left end; so yes, the full-score tool may overhang the edge, by
its handle only, as it started, with 7 mm to spare for laying it back); every other point of the tool inside the top's
outline; and every point between 10 mm below and 40 mm above the stone's surface (the wand's end may reach past the
basin's rim but not hang into the basin). The tool must rest there by itself: a real tool that tips off the edge has
dropped (score 0). With 75 mm past the edge a tool that passes the set-up check (above, under the tool) still has its balance point at
least 3 mm over the stone; the demo lays it back with 68 mm (lot A) and 60 mm (lot B) past.

| Score | End state | How it is read in a real trial |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | The hair is out of the drain and at the countertop's height (on the pad or off it), and the whole tool lies on the countertop (as defined above) | Top photo; a ruler along the top's end for the handle's overhang; a straightedge laid across the rim with a ruler under it for anything reaching into the basin; the scale for the hair on the counter |
| 0.5 | The hair is out of the drain and at the countertop's height, but the tool does not lie wholly on the countertop: part of it down in the basin, more than 75 mm of the handle past the edge, or another part past the top's outline | Same |
| 0.25 | The hair is out of the drain (outside the flange, or 10 mm or more above the flange's top) but not at the countertop's height: left in the basin, or dropped past the countertop's edge | Same; the scale for the hair in the basin or on the floor |
| 0 | The hair is still in the drain, or anything above with a gripper within 10 mm of the tool, or the tool moving | Torch down the drain; the scale for what is fished out |

Trials are run on lots A and B and averaged.

### The simulation scorer (task.py)

- `observe()` runs after every control step and keeps the trial record: whether the tool has dropped off the vanity
  (`off_vanity`), the hair's state (below), whether both grippers are clear (`hands_clear`: every point sampled 3 mm
  apart on every gripper face more than 13 mm from every box of the tool, i.e. 10 mm plus the sampling), stillness
  and how long the state has held.
- **Declared rule for the hair (`hair_step`)**, the approximation of the wound clump. The hair is soft and the wand
  passes into it, so the clump is drawn but does not collide; what it does to the tool is applied as a force.
  1. *Snag.* The pad's barbs point back toward the handle, so they catch hair only on the way back. The clump is
     snagged when the pad's centre has gone at least 10 mm into it (32 mm or more below the flange's top, inside the
     32 mm bore; the clump fills it from 22 to 52 mm below the flange's top) and is then drawn back up at least 8 mm
     from its deepest point without leaving the clump. Poking the pad in and lifting it straight out in one move, or
     leaving the clump first, snags nothing.
  2. *Resistance while it is drawn out.* From the snag until the clump's centre (on the pad) reaches the flange's top,
     the clump is still wound on the stem and rod and packed in the bore, and it resists: every control step a force
     is set on the pad (split over its two links, so it acts at the pad's centre; `apply_hair_force`, through the
     simulator's external-force call) pointing down the drain's axis, against the pad's rise: 3 N when the pad
     rises at 4 mm/s or faster (its rise taken over the last 0.2 s, four control steps), in proportion below that,
     its target falling to nothing once the pad has not risen over the last 0.2 s (like friction, it does not hold a
     pad at rest); the force eases toward that target with a 0.15 s time constant, so it cannot switch on and off
     from one step to the next and set the light pad chattering. So it builds to half within about 0.1 s of the pad
     starting to rise, and fades over about half a second once the pad stops or moves down (still above 1 N for
     about 0.3 s). Its equal reaction acts on the drain, which is
     fixed to the basin. The wand carries the load back to the handle, so the holding hand has to pull the wand out
     through it and the grip has to hold the handle against it.
  3. *Breakaway.* The barbs lose the hair, which stays wound in the drain (back in its place) and must be snagged
     again, if the pad is jerked up faster than 0.10 m/s (its rise averaged over 0.2 s), if it is pushed back down
     more than 5 mm below the highest point it has drawn the clump to, or if it reaches the flange's top before
     gaining 15 mm of new height (above the highest point reached since the snag; the rise from any snag is 22 mm or
     more) with at least half the load (1.5 N) acting: crept out slower than about 2 mm/s, the hair stays. Only new
     height counts, so moving the pad up and down in place adds nothing.
  4. *Free.* Reaching the flange's top otherwise, the clump is out of the throat and free; from then on it rides on
     the pad (`hair_pose`, written every step in `_put_hair`) and the force stops. Only a freed clump scores.

  The values are estimates, not measurements. The 3 N is the order of the pull carried by a few strands caught on
  the barbs: a wet human hair breaks at roughly 0.5-1 N (Robbins, *Chemical and Physical Behavior of Human Hair*),
  and the wound turns must slide along the stem, over the rod and past the fins. It is a limit on what the barbs can
  hold, not a measurement of a wound clump; the real clump's pull-out force has not been measured. The 0.10 m/s jerk
  limit and the 5 mm push-back stand for barbs that hold hair only under a steady backward pull (the maker's
  directions: "Carefully pull snake and debris from the drain"). Not simulated: the tangle itself,
  hair left behind, the force varying along the way, and the clump catching on the fins or the head once free.
- `tier()` gives 0.25 / 0.5 / 1.0 as in the table (`hair_out_of_drain`, `hair_on_counter`, `tool_on_counter`), and
  0 unless the clump was freed; `score()` returns it only when the record is valid, the tool it is given is the one
  the last step observed, the tool never dropped, the hands are clear and everything has held still for 1 s. If the
  force cannot be set in a step, the trial's record is spoiled and it scores 0.

## What the simulation simplifies (fidelity)

Rigid bodies (PhysX). The wand's last 128 mm is eight 16 mm links on spring hinges (0.2 N m/rad each, an estimate
for a 3 mm polypropylene strip) that bend in one plane; the first 177 mm is straight and fixed in the handle. The
links weigh 2 g each (a real wand is lighter) to keep the solver stable. The hair is a declared rule (above), an
approximation: it does not collide; it is snagged only by drawing the pad back through it after going 10 mm in; while
it is dragged off the stem and up the bore, an estimated 3 N resists the pad's rise (applied as a force on the pad,
friction-like); jerked, pushed back or crept out, it stays. Once free it comes out whole on the pad, passes the
stopper's head without catching and cannot be wiped off; its wet tangle, a varying pull and hair left behind are not
simulated. What is simulated is the access: the head is wider than the bore and the fins stand in it, so the pad
reaches the clump only by going in under the head's edge and bending down the bore beside the stem. The handle's 360-degree spin (the
maker's step to wind more hair) and water are not simulated. Stopper fixed open. Vanity on the robot's table, its
cabinet cut there.

The scripted demo plays fixed waypoints from the published layout (the lot it is given); it reads nothing from the
simulator. It closes firmly on the round grip (about 40 N a finger, within the gripper's 50 N), so the held tool keeps
the pose it had as it lay on the stone, 1.6-1.75 degrees off the grip's pose (measured on the GPU in both lots; with
half that squeeze it turned a further 1-4 degrees in the fingers when lifted, varying from trial to trial, and missed
the gap under the head once in two trials). At the insertion slope the wand's hinged end droops 20 mm (measured). The
demo aims both of these the other way. It feeds the pad deep into the clump (17 mm in), holds 1.5 s, then draws the
wand back along its own line over 3 s and out from under the head over 2.5 s: the snag comes about 8 mm into the draw,
and the 3 N load acts until the clump reaches the flange's top; under it the tool turns a few degrees in the fingers.
Laying the tool back, it brings the tool round with the wand's end tipped up 8 degrees, so the drooping end crosses the
basin's rim above the stone, and turns it 3 degrees against a turn in the grip.

## Recorded runs

Scripted demo of this version (`--policy yam_scripted`, both lots, one run of the final source): 1.0 in lot A and
1.0 in lot B. In both
lots the pad went 17 mm into the clump and held there; drawing back, the barbs snagged the hair 8-9 mm into the draw
(28.2 s in lot A, 28.1 s in lot B). The hair's load then acted for 3.0-3.1 s while the wand was drawn out, averaging
2.3 N (lot A) and 2.2 N (lot B) over the snagged steps as it built and eased with the pad's rise; the clump gained
30 mm and 22 mm of new height under at least half the load (15 mm needed), and it came free at the flange's top at
31.3 s and 31.1 s, with no breakaway. Under the load the tool turned about 5 degrees in the fingers. The tool was laid back with 68 mm (lot A)
and 60 mm (lot B) of handle past the top's end, the hair on the pad over the stone, and both trials ended at 41.2 s
after the 1 s still hold. (In the simulation lot B's tool settles 2.4 mm short of its mark when the layout is set.)

Development runs of this version: with half the squeeze, the tool turned 1-4 degrees in the fingers when lifted, by a
different amount each trial, and in one of two trials the wand's end rode up onto the head instead of under it; with
the load set from the pad's speed each step without easing, the light pad chattered, the load acted in only some
steps, and the hair slipped off at the flange's top (a breakaway, scored 0). An earlier run of the final demo, on
the source before the credit was limited to new height (a scoring change only), gave the same trajectory and 1.0 in
both lots.

Dream-1 (both lots, same final source as the demo): 0.0 and 0.0, both at the step limit with no refusal. It lowered
both grippers in front of the countertop, the left one near the handle's end, and never picked up the tool. Earlier
Dream-1 runs, all 0.0 in both lots at the step limit with no refusal, with the same behaviour: one on the source before
the scoring-only change to new height, and one on the first version of this task.

## Ways round the task, and what they score

| Route | Result |
|---|---|
| Grip the hair with the fingers | Impossible: the hair is 22 to 52 mm down a 32 mm bore under a 42 mm head |
| Lift the stopper out first | Impossible: it is held by its pivot rod |
| Push the wand straight down beside the head | The head covers the bore; the wand lands on the head or the flange: hair stays, 0 |
| Poke the pad only into the top of the clump (less than 10 mm), or lift it straight out without drawing it back through the clump | Not snagged: 0 |
| Yank the snagged hair out (faster than 0.10 m/s, averaged over 0.2 s), push the pad back down more than 5 mm after the snag, or creep it out slower than 2 mm/s to dodge the load | The barbs lose the hair, which stays in the drain: 0 unless snagged again and drawn out properly |
| Snag the hair, jiggle the pad up and down in place under the load (strokes under 5 mm), then creep it out | Only new height gained under the load counts: the hair slips off at the flange's top, 0 |
| Lay the tool back with its handle hanging further past the top's end (more than 75 mm) | Not lying on the countertop: 0.5 (a real tool may tip off: 0) |
| Pull the hair out, leave it with the tool in the basin | 0.25 |
| Pull the hair out, leave the tool hanging into the basin | 0.25 or 0.5 |
| Throw the tool on the floor (even after a full end state) | 0 for the whole trial |
| Lay the tool down still held | 0 |

The near misses in `near_misses.py` play these and the tier boundaries through the scorer.

The task needs one hand; the demo uses the left arm only. A second hand is free to hold the sleeve still, as the
maker's directions describe for the spin step.

## How this differs from close tasks

- Board tasks on drains are plumbing assembly (refitting a J-bend, dry-fitting a slip coupling, sawing a drain pipe),
  fishing a spoon out of a disposal with tongs (reaching into an open opening), and reconnecting a pop-up stopper's
  pivot rod under the basin. None feeds a flexible tool past an obstruction into a concealed bore and draws debris
  out with it.
- TK-MTD23U cleared a blocked washbasin with a plunger (pressure, not a tool that hooks and removes debris); it was
  closed because its fibre-and-soap blockage could not be reproduced. Here the clog is a weighed hair bundle wound to
  a stated place, and the score is the weighed hair that comes out.
- BEHAVIOR's `clean_a_drain` wipes mildew off a sink with a rag and soap; it has no stopper, tool or hidden clog.

## Resetting a real trial

1. Under the basin, undo the pivot nut, draw the pivot rod back out of the stopper's eye and lift the stopper out.
   Clear any hair left in the drain (torch and tweezers); blot and air-dry it for 1 h and weigh it.
2. Make the clump the same way each time: 0.50 g (+-0.02 g) of 150 mm strands cut from one 100% human-hair weft,
   soaked in water for 1 min and drained for 30 s. Wind it three snug turns round the stopper's stem among and just
   below its fins, tucking the ends under the last turn, and check with a ruler on the stopper that it spans 42 to
   72 mm below the head's underside (22 to 52 mm below the flange's top once the stopper is in and open). Spare
   stoppers can be wound ahead, so a reset is a stopper swap (about 5 min). Lower the stopper back in, push the pivot
   rod back through its eye so the rod passes through the hanging hair, and refit the pivot nut. The rod through the
   eye sets the stopper's turn: check its fins against the marks made on the flange at the first set-up (30 and 120
   degrees from +x). The real clump's pull-out force is not measured (the simulation uses the estimate above).
3. Push the lift rod so the stopper stands fully open (head 20 mm above the flange).
4. Fit a new wand (an 18 in refill trimmed to 305 mm at its handle end) to the handle (sleeve up, wand into its
   channel, sleeve down to lock).
5. Lay the tool on the countertop's front strip at its lot's marks: along the front edge, wand to the right, the
   wand meeting the handle 65 mm (lot A) or 75 mm (lot B) in from the top's left end. Let go and check that it rests
   there untouched for 10 s, as the simulated tool does (see "Balance with the handle over the edge").
6. Both arms to their ready pose, grippers open.
