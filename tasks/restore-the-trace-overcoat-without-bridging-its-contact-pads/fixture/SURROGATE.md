# Proposed optical stand-in qualification

The reference job uses MG Chemicals 422B. The simulation uses the declared finite transfer law, not a measured material calibration. The following recipe is a reproducible candidate for optical correspondence testing before any shared-rig scoring. It has not been prepared, tested or approved for a particular rig. It is not a protective coating and does not reproduce cure or electrical insulation.

At 25 +/- 2 C, weigh 60.00 g Sigma-Aldrich G5516 glycerol, 39.99 g deionized water and 0.010 g Kingscote Bright Dyes FLT Yellow/Green Liquid product 106001 using a 1 mg-resolution balance. Mix gently for 2 minutes, cover and let bubbles settle for 10 minutes. Record ingredient lot numbers, actual masses, temperature and preparation time. Dispense 5.00 mL into the original service cup; use a clean dry MG859 and a separate, identically prepared unpowered native board. Use the same batch for both layouts on the day of preparation. This 60% nominal glycerol recipe is an estimate chosen to make a visible viscous liquid, not a claim of matching 422B viscosity, wetting, surface tension, adhesion or spreading. Do not silently tune the recipe to obtain a passing task.

The [glycerol SDS](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/sds/SIGMA/G5516) identifies G5516 and its handling information. Kingscote's [product page](https://www.kingscotechemicals.com/products/tracer-dyes/liquid/yellow-green/) identifies 106001 as a fluorescent water tracer; its [SDS](https://www.kingscotechemicals.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/FLT-YG-Liquid.pdf) notes staining and possible eye irritation. Those documents do not establish compatibility of this mixture with a shared gripper, board, camera or bench. Rig-specific material permission and cleanup arrangements are still prerequisites to physical testing.

Qualification records must include five repetitions each of: a dry stroke, a wet hover 2 mm above the surface, a 1 mm nominal-compression stroke, a 3 mm overcompressed stroke, and repeated strokes without redipping. Use the same 40-degree rake, native heading and 14 mm trace length as the declared demonstration, with its two 4 s strokes and a reload between them; record actual tool and board poses, not only commands. Separately record 1, 2 and 4 s tip immersions, brush mass before/after loading and after each stroke, and cup mass loss with a 1 mg balance and measured mixture density. Capture registered UV-A macro and side views at the specified resolution, including the native pad masks. Retain the actual footprint, native-trace coverage, pad area, bridges and depletion sequence alongside the simulated counterpart with the same measured poses.

Correspondence remains unresolved until dry/hover passes show no persistent film, wet passes visibly transfer finite material, repeated strokes deplete, overcompression expands the footprint, and the actual and simulated pad-fouling/bridge and score-tier classifications agree in every recorded repetition. Report all volume and footprint discrepancies; do not infer physical transfer from the recipe or select only agreeing trials. Failure calls for a documented material/model revision followed by new validation; it does not waive keepouts or the gap rule. The current native simulation evidence is unchanged by this proposed physical protocol.
