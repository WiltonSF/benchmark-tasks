# Free a soft-robot actuator wall from its vise-clamped casting core

With the core held in the vise, grip the silicone actuator wall with both hands, turn it a quarter turn to break it free of the core, withdraw it along the core, then leave it intact in the parts tray. The instruction asks for both hands, as the cited procedure supports the cast front and rear; the simulation does not enforce two hands (one hand can free the bond, see the one-hand controls).

## Who does this and why

Soft-robot builders remove every cast actuator from its core before capping and testing it (Soft Robotics Toolkit, fiber-reinforced actuator, Step 3b). The core's end goes in a vise, the actuator is turned a quarter turn to break it free, and it is drawn off along the core. Pull too hard on one end, pinch a cut end, or lever the wall sideways, and the pressure wall tears and the part is scrap. This is a regular step of building soft robot hardware, so the category is Impact on AI timelines. The robot must hold a thin, slippery elastomer part firmly enough to overcome a 170 mm squeeze-fit without crushing or tearing it, then let go of it in a tray.

## Scoring with simple tools

Damage (new cut of 0.5 mm or more, or failed integrity check), a withdrawal begun before the quarter turn (read from a longitudinal witness line drawn across wall and core: the line on the wall must be at least 60 degrees round from the core's line before the wall moves 3 mm along it; side video, frame-stepped), a core departure beyond 1 mm or 2 degrees, missing loaded jaw contact, an off-bench drop, or invalid state/contact updates latches score 0 for the whole trial. End-state requirements are separate: score 0 while any wall remains on the core or within 5 mm of it, while either hand remains within 30 mm, or until the release/support/stillness conditions below hold. Those end-state failures can recover if no whole-trial failure has occurred.

Otherwise, with the wall released, resting and both hands at least 30 mm away for at least 1 s:

| Score | Final state | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| 0.25 | wall resting on the bench outside the tray | overhead photo |
| 0.50 | wall entirely inside the tray, on its floor, but released and still for less than 2 s | frame-stepped video |
| 0.75 | inside the tray and released 2 s, centre more than 10 mm from the tray centre in x or y | ruler, photo |
| 1.00 | inside the tray, released 2 s, centre within 10 mm of the tray centre in x and y | ruler, photo |

In simulation, stillness allows at most1 mm displacement of monitored part points, linear speed at most8 mm/s and angular speed at most0.15 rad/s. Bench or tray-floor support has a2 mm height tolerance. Hand sampling uses a3 mm numerical guard over the30 mm clearance. The simulation compares the core to its clamped pose at the trial's first observation, after the reset settle. The tray counts only upright (floor within 5 degrees of level) and resting on the bench. In simulation the damage rules below stand in for the integrity check.

The [NewAge material catalogue](https://newageindustries.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/NewAge_Silicone_Catalog.pdf) gives typical Silcon Shore A50 +/-5 and Die B tear resistance 120 ppi (about 21 N/mm). These are material test figures. The simulation derives its damage forces from them openly: 21.0 N/mm x 1.5875 mm wall = 33.3 N per hand link, and half that (16.7 N) at a cut end (see "Damage" below). This is a derivation, not a measured onset for this wall. The supplier warns that sharp fittings and unlined clamps can tear the tube, which supports treating cut ends as the more sensitive place. [Wacker's mouldmaking catalogue](https://www.wacker.com/h/medias/7892-EN.pdf) lists M4601 at Shore A28 and tear resistance >30 N/mm, whereas Silcon is Shore A50 and about21 N/mm. The tube is harder and has a lower published tear figure; matching the cast wall cannot be inferred from these datasheets. The matching tube/rod has three lab pull readings, 11, 11, 13 N (mean 11.7 N); no cast comparison or damage-onset test is supplied.

## Simulation model and declared values

Matching-specimen tube/rod pull readings are 11, 11, 13 N (mean 11.7 N), measured by a soft-robotics lab on the matching specimen (Nov 2026). These are the supplied real pull figures. No quarter-turn torque, damage threshold, cast comparison, rate/wetting record or batch qualification accompanies them; other physical parameters remain estimates.

- Full-engagement withdrawal target: 11.7 N from the lab readings above. The previous 10 N engineering estimate is retained only as background: Gent's Shore relation gives about 2.46 MPa at Shore A50 ([Dow's modulus note](https://www.dow.com/documents/11/11-3716-01-durometer-hardness-for-silicones.pdf?iframe=true)). Radial interference0.15 mm / bore radius6.35 mm gives2.4% hoop strain; wall1.59 mm gives about92 N/m hoop tension and14 kPa contact pressure. Over the cylindrical contact area pi x13 mm x170 mm, this is about98 N integrated normal load. An assumed real sliding coefficient0.10 gives about10 N withdrawal and0.065 Nm twist. Taking0.05–0.20 instead gives a5–20 N sensitivity interval, not a measured confidence interval. Bore tolerance, wetting and material variation can dominate this calculation.
- Native contact model. The12.7 mm bore interferes with the13 mm core. The core's tube-contact span uses PhysX implicit compliant contacts: penalty stiffness125,000 N/m/contact, damping0.5 N s/m/contact, force units; core and wall depenetration caps2 m/s. The jaws retain rigid contact and a1 m/s cap. The numerical friction coefficient 0.15 is min-combined at this interface. It is a solver calibration parameter, not a measured coefficient; the actual native load must be checked against the 11.7 N reference in both layouts. Native normal pressure and the resulting resistance vary with the contact configuration. The coefficient and penalty stiffness are estimates, not measured material properties. Earlier traces varied from about 75 to 156 N integrated normal before grasp, with different initial wall roll. Reset now re-enrols the wall and core at their declared poses before the final 0.5 s native settle, clearing inherited roll at reset only. Contact-patch area/profile and elastic response are approximate. During a trial there is no task-applied wrench, extra contact-generated load, pose write or proximity trigger. The one task-held element is the bond below: a native PhysX constraint that only resists the hands, which the task code switches off when its twist reaches the release angle. Pose re-enrolment is confined to reset before the final native settle. PhysX supplies the complete contact force, friction, bond reaction and equal reaction on the core.
- Vise. Only native contacts with the lower jaw and driven upper jaw hold the free core. The upper jaw is a position drive of 500 N/mm stiffness (capped at 1000 N) set 1 mm past first contact, standing in for a hand-tightened vise screw; the resulting jaw load is reported below. Jaw friction is 0.90, a value for serrated steel jaws on a steel rod, matching the named Yost BV-4's serrated steel jaw pads. Both were raised (from 200 N/mm and 0.60) after the core spun in the jaws under 1.7-1.9 N m of bond torque; a 2 mm target sank the core 1.6 mm into the jaws and was dropped. All are estimates. Both jaws must remain loaded; any core departure beyond1 mm or2 degrees, including axial spin, latches0 at physics substeps after the trial starts.
- Damage, derived openly from the stock datasheet and tube dimensions (estimates, not measured onsets). NewAge gives Silcon Die B tear strength 120 ppi = 21.0 N/mm; the nominal wall is 1/16 in = 1.5875 mm. A finger pad pressed through the full wall section is taken to start a cut at 21.0 x 1.5875 = 33.3 N (CUT_N). This uses a tear-propagation figure as a cut-onset force; it is a stated derivation, not a measured pinch-damage onset. Rules: (a) any one hand link (a finger pad or the gripper body) pressing the wall above 33.3 N for more than 0.1 s; (b) one hand's links together above 66.7 N (both pads at CUT_N) for more than 0.1 s; (c) any one hand link pressing within 5 mm of a cut end above 16.7 N, half of CUT_N because a cut end is supported on one side only (an assumption); (d) the wall forced more than 4 mm off axis or tilted more than 15 degrees while more than 10 mm remains engaged AND the current native bore contact load is at least 33.3 N (the interference preload alone is 82-105 N, so while engaged this rule is in effect geometric); (e) the bond broken by pull or lever before it is freed by the turn (below). Forces are current native contact normals (at least 0.001 N per point and separation at most 0.1 mm to count as a reading). Previous contact cannot trigger a new tear after contact is lost. Any event latches 0. These stand in for the real integrity test.
- Bond (first release, Step 3b quarter turn). At the start of every trial a native PhysX revolute constraint joins the wall to the core along the core axis, with contacts between them still active. It carries axial pull, radial shear and lever loads rigidly until PhysX breaks it at 292 N or 6 N m. The task latches a tear whenever the bonded wall moves more than 0.5 mm along or across the core or tilts 1.5 degrees on it, whether PhysX has broken the constraint or the constraint is merely yielding under load; this displacement gate can fire well below 292 N (in the one-hand control a one-hand pull displaced the bonded wall 0.7 mm). Twist about the axis acts on the constraint's native torsional drive, 1.77 N m/rad, so the bond's resisting torque rises to 1.85 N m at 60 degrees of twist; at 60 degrees the task code switches the constraint off for good and only the native bore friction (the 11.7 N reference) resists. This models a bond that shears progressively round the bore as the wall turns. Basis (estimate, no published value): a silicone/steel bond of 43.0 kPa shear over the 12.7 mm x 170 mm bore (6,783 mm2) gives 43.0 kPa x 6,783 mm2 x 6.35 mm = 1.85 N m and 1.85 / 6.35 mm = 292 N; the 6 N m lever limit and the 60 degree release angle are estimates. The value was raised in steps (0.85, 1.40, 2.60, 2.20, then 1.85 N m) after simulation controls: one hand freed the 0.85 and 1.40 N m bonds, because in this rigid 12-panel model the pads also press on the panel flats (a round real tube would give less torque per newton of grip); one hand slipped at about 1.66 N m with 60 N of grip; and at 2.60 N m two hands spun the core in the 200 N vise model, so the vise drive was raised; at 2.20 N m two hands stalled at about 2.06 N m; the bond was then set to 1.85 N m, between the one-hand slip torque and the two-hand stall torque. The value is set from the simulated gripper and vise; the one-hand result is reported below. The bond only resists the hands: if they let go before release, its elastic twist turns the wall back towards its bonded position, as a partly sheared elastic bond would; it never moves the wall otherwise. It is a single named constant (BOND_SHEAR_PA).
- Exterior traction. The estimated dry silicone coefficients 1.2 static / 1.0 dynamic use [Lee et al., Materials 15 (2022), 3262](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/15/9/3262) only as an analogue: Sylgard 184 PDMS against a 1 mm steel ball, 0.1 N normal load and 16 mm/s reciprocation. Dry friction initially exceeded 1.0. This is a different material/contact pair at much smaller load and does not establish NewAge tube/YAM-pad traction.
- Geometry. The wall is pink in simulation for visibility; the commercial stock is natural/translucent. The wall consists of12 rigid box panels, nominal12.7 mm bore and16.1 mm across outside flats (about16.7 mm across polygon corners). The round core uses24 facets (13 mm across flats, about13.11 mm across corners). Compliant contact permits the nominal interference; continuum wall bending, stretching, adhesion and leaks are absent. Silicone14 g and steel231 g masses are volume/density estimates.
- Demo. Both hands grip the wall (29 N per pad commanded, 23-26 N measured while twisting) and twist it together; the turn is commanded to 90 degrees and the bond lets go at 60 degrees (the wall reached 97 degrees in lot A and 73 degrees in lot B). Each hand then eases to about 15 N per pad and they regrasp one hand at a time at a reachable pulling angle, withdraw it along the clamped core, then release it in the tray. One hand supports the turned wall while the other opens and reorients; the wall is not deliberately rolled back. The demo's peak single-link load, 34.9 N, briefly exceeds the 33.3 N limit and is not a tear only because it lasts less than 0.1 s. IPA is applied during reset, not robot-dispensed or simulated as liquid.
- Invalid data. Malformed or non-finite active state/contact readings and stopped/stale physics or observations latch0. Unused contact-buffer slots are not read.
