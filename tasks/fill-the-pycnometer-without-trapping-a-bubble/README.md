# Fill the pycnometer without trapping a bubble

Status: implemented in Isaac Lab-Arena with a declared fluid proxy. On the final source, the reference demo scores A 1.0 and B 1.0, and Dream-1 scores A 0.0 and B 0.0. No hardware result is claimed.

Laboratory staff remove trapped air before density weighing. Category **Other**: a real laboratory instrument procedure. A 0.25 mL bubble in a 50 mL bottle displaces 0.5% of the sample volume. The [Eisco PH0122 instructions](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81E3ZMTn0dL.pdf) prescribe filling approximately one-third up the ground neck, avoiding bubbles, bringing the bottle to temperature in a bath, inserting the capillary stopper, drying the exterior and weighing. The [PH0122B product page](https://www.eiscolabs.com/products/ph0122b) specifies 50 mL borosilicate 3.3, flat bottom, perforated stopper, 50.8 mm diameter, 69.85 mm bare height and 101.6 mm assembled height. These pages were opened Nov 24. This water-only manipulation trial does not certify a density assay or ASTM/ISO compliance.

Figures **obtained by phone from a technician who works with this product (Nov 2026)**: 20–40% of first fills trap about 0.25 mL under the conical ground joint/shoulder; dry bottle about 40 g, stopper about 15 g; stopper seating about 200 g of finger pressure plus a slight twist. The recovery is gentle tilt, rotation and tapping to walk the bubble into the neck, topping up with de-aired water past the ground joint, then lowering the stopper straight down and wiping its tip. The simulation uses 40 g/15 g, one 0.25 mL shoulder pocket and a 1.962 N minimum native stopper-hand input plus net axial glass compression (0.200 kg×9.81). These phone figures do not supply a bubble adhesion force, internal dimensions, capillary bore, wet friction or a timed reset series; those remain declared estimates.

The start reproduces the normal first-fill error described by the technician. Fill using the maker's procedure, gently top up down the neck wall, and meter one 0.25 mL air charge beneath the shoulder with a graduated syringe and blunt bent needle. The syringe is a preparation tool for repeatable replication of that error; it is not a step in a density assay. Enroll exactly one visible shoulder bubble and no neck or capillary pockets. Stage it on the shoulder side facing the robot bases (toward -x, azimuth 180° ±30°, 0° pointing away from the arms). No twelve-bubble pattern, one-degree window or start-volume estimate through curved glass is needed. The syringe is removed before the trial.

The robot tilts and rotates the bottle, tops up at the submerged rest, and counterholds it while inserting the stopper straight down with a slight twist. Bath top-up is a disclosed water-only simplification of the technician's wash-bottle top-up. Tapping, wiping and an analytical temperature bath are outside the simulated manipulation. No fixture clears air or seats the stopper.

## Score with simple tools

Score 0 permanently for broken or lost glass, water lost beyond the catch area, rig damage, or observations that cannot establish a valid trial. Simulation also zeros on missing/nonfinite body or hand data, bad clock/inventory, or sampled fingertip effort/contact load above 40 N. Tiny incidental droplets within the catch area are allowed. Declared drainage is tested at the actual mouth and capillary overflow at the actual capillary exit, vertically projected into the 650 x 650 mm catch area; outside-catch discharge is permanently latched even if the bottle is later refilled. Ballistic splash trajectories are not resolved. Moving the unmanipulated bath more than 5 mm or rotating it more than 2° invalidates the simulated trial. No state is credited while an arm holds either scored object: the bottle must be on the bath floor/rest, inspection pad or starting shelf; both pieces must be supported, still below 3 mm/s translation and 0.03 rad/s rotation for 1 s, and every gripper and finger more than 5 mm clear. The simulation uses a conservative 8 mm distance from full face-sampled geometry and requires housing/forearm-to-glass contact load at most 0.05 N for release. Each glass piece must also have at least 0.01 N actual contact load against the kit or the other glass piece throughout the 1 s hold; arms never count as supports. Bottle height must match a permitted support surface. Missing/nonfinite support data gives 0. Insufficient hold earns 0 at that instant; it does not preserve an earlier peak.

Exterior water **never counts as interior water**. A missing interior volume below the 50 mL reference also counts against the air tiers, so pouring out water cannot leave a nominally bubble-free high score. After robot release and the 1 s hold, the evaluator may remove the closed assembly for measurement, blot only the exterior and stopper sides with tissue, and weigh it without moving its stopper or wicking the capillary. This is measurement, not an unscored robot completion step. Repeat exterior blot/weigh until successive readings differ by at most 0.02 g. If that cannot be achieved, no full mass credit. Record the before/after weights. The simulation computes mass from interior water only; its exterior-film inventory is excluded, so a wet underfilled bottle cannot compensate for missing internal water.

Use two orthogonal views with backlighting, a ruler in each focal plane and at least 20 pixels/mm at the bottle; the station overview alone cannot certify sub-mL air. At a threshold use measured volume PLUS uncertainty; overlap earns only the lower tier. Unknown air visibility gives no valid air tier. Inspect the capillary separately; capillary air also counts.

Calibrate the 0.15 mL overflow witness by filming a 0.15 mL syringe discharge through the same capillary against a ruled card before trials; collect output on preweighed tissue in an isolated small receiver when accessible, accepting only a lower mass bound of 0.15 g. Full credit needs visible water exiting the stopper tip during actual insertion, not liquid arriving from another source or just wet glass. The robot's stopper may be grasped at its side but may not occlude its capillary opening. ## Declared physics and controls

No CFD or resolved wetting. A single pocket starts at the upper shoulder (nominal polar 30°, azimuth 180°). Its unmeasured retention force is nominally estimated as contact-line perimeter times water surface tension:2 pi×3.908 mm×72.74 mN/m≈1.786 mN for 0.25 mL. The 3.908 mm contact-line radius is the equivalent-volume sphere radius, an estimate of the available perimeter, not an established upper bound on real adhesion; actual contact angle and hysteresis are unmeasured. [IAPWS2014](https://iapws.org/public/documents/CH-L9/Surf-H2O-2014.pdf), opened Nov 24, provides sigma=235.8 tau^1.256(1-0.625 tau)mN/m, tau=1-T/647.096; at 293.15 K this gives 72.74 mN/m. This supplies water surface tension, not measured glass adhesion. Retention scales with the cube root of pocket volume (line radius), while buoyancy scales with volume. Tangential buoyancy above that estimated resistance depins it. Migration speed is force/0.025 Ns/m, capped 0.05 m/s, along the inner wall. A pocket fully exposed above the remaining gravity-plane water surface also vents; draining/refilling in the catch area is a physically valid alternative. Once mobile and within the neck aperture, air vents at estimated 0.6 mL/s through a clear open neck. This can happen in air, but creates no water. Open upright submerged neck top-up supplies the missing volume at estimated 10 mL/s. Draining follows the gravity-plane retained capacity from 3 mm inner samples, capped 12 mL/s; a dry capillary uses a Poiseuille leakage estimate (radius 0.7 mm, length 52 mm, viscosity 1 mPa·s). These are replaceable named constants near task.py's top, not technician adhesion/flow measurements. The model is not evidence of physical reset repeatability.

The 72-case sensitivity sweep in near_misses.py covers 0.20/0.25/0.30 mL, polar 15/30/35° and eight azimuths. Upright no-op keeps air pinned, upright immersion alone does not clear it, a tilt toward the visible bubble's radial direction then neck-up vents it, and top-up/reseat reaches the modeled reference. This checks a range of visible recovery motions, not the fixed demo in 72 randomized scenes. Fixed A/B demos use the nominal normal and two diagonal 40° rocks. A separate 45-case prescribed-state control checks those same fixed 40° rocks over 0.23/0.25/0.27 mL, polar 15/30/35° and five azimuths from 150° to 210°. These are model controls, not robot episodes. Other controls cover untreated-air stoppering, inverted drainage/dry-pocket exposure/refill, blocked capillary, seal/overflow contact, both-hand support, exterior-film exclusion, stale/nonfinite state and permanent outside-catch spills. Prescribed-state controls are not robot demonstrations.

The stopper's swept volume expels water only from actual excess inventory; pocket volume is never deleted by seating. Only contact-established wet-seal flow counts as capillary overflow. Its 0.2 N wet-contact proxy is a declared estimate, rounded above the 15 g stopper weight 0.147 N; it routes fluid but does not qualify full seating. The technician's 200 g is final seating pressure, not a constant hydraulic drag across the entire insertion. Full credit independently requires at least 1.962 N measured **net compressive axial contact (normal plus friction) on both glass bodies AND actual axial input from the stopper-holding fingertips**, at insertion depth≥12.65 mm and under actual opposite-hand pinches. This uses native collision/friction forces and their equal/opposite reactions; no artificial stopper wrench, pose constraint, spring or seating motor is applied. Lateral contact magnitude alone, tension, unloaded crossing and a 1.961 N press cannot satisfy the witness. Actual finger input is the opposite of total normal-plus-friction contact force on the assigned stopper-hand fingertips, projected down the bottle axis. Glass reaction can include stopper weight and impact inertia; it cannot substitute for the 1.962 N finger-input witness. Side closing effort and passive upward support do not qualify. Proxy depth is 63 mm minus the stopper-tip axial offset from the bottle base frame; the full reference is 13.15 mm. Thus 12.65 mm is 0.5 mm short of that reference, not a maker insertion-depth specification. In hardware, qualify force within 0.5 mm of the same-pair fully seated reference using the calibrated ruler/photo; the final released seat still has the separate 2 mm tolerance. 

Full credit still needs at least 0.15 mL capillary overflow under opposite-hand pinches:each body pad≥1.3 N, each stopper pad≥0.3 N. A wet seal without the full native seating press earns at most 0.75. Flow without a seal is bypass; blocked overcapacity invalidates permanently. Native contact friction supplies insertion/withdrawal resistance. Wet grip and actual pressure/seat relationship must be qualified on real glass. Use a calibrated low-range spring gauge during pair enrollment and a calibrated force readout for a real pressure claim; record readout uncertainty and method, do not infer 200 g from a video alone. No physical force-telemetry capability or instrumented trial is claimed here; the kit's measurement allowance includes a low-range force-gauge allowance, not a price quote.

Water updates actual bottle mass, COM and inertia, treated rigidly entrained. Buoyancy/drag capped 1.2/0.6 N and passive rotational drag are opposed by equal bath reaction. Gripper pads have estimated 2 mm torsional contact patches (minimum 1.5 mm), not attachments. Slosh, splash trajectories, arm-water loads, bath-level drop during transfers, detailed contact angles and gas compression are omitted. The bath waterline is fixed at 80 mm in the proxy. The blue overflow stream is a qualitative flow marker; numeric discharge comes from conserved inventory. The scripted demo lowers the stopper with a slight 20° twist split between initial lowering and the final loaded press while the other hand keeps counterholding. The 20° command is a reference-motion choice; the phone figure specifies a slight twist without a measured angle. Native wet contact friction resists twisting; its torque has not been calibrated, and no measured torque capability is claimed.

On the current host, IsaacSim 6.1 reports normal force separately from friction. The observer sums detailed normal contacts and friction anchors using their own copied counts/start indices, then projects the total onto the bottle axis. It preserves the original normal-contact threshold in addition to the net pressure threshold. Missing, nonfinite, malformed, saturated or aggregate-inconsistent buffers invalidate the trial. The glass and bath use 156 position and 4 velocity solver iterations, authored before native scene creation; these are numerical settings. [NVIDIA’s TGS limitations guidance](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/ovphysx/0.6.4/guides/limitations.html), opened Nov 24, advises zero or very few velocity iterations for drive stability. With the stopper wedged in the taper, its pose held within 0.001 mm and 0.0002 rad per 0.05 s step while the reported body velocity stayed near 0.03 m/s and up to 1 rad/s, with zero or four velocity iterations. The 1 s stillness check therefore measures pose change over each 0.05 s control step, as a camera would: bottle and stopper must each stay below 3 mm/s and 0.03 rad/s, the same limits as before. A 4 mm/s drift fails this check; a stationary pose with a nonzero reported velocity passes. The numerical trial retains all geometry, physical coefficients and scoring limits; it is not a physical damping or force adjustment. The contact views allocate 8,192 records (16,384 for the fingertip load view). Saturation still invalidates; these are sensor storage sizes, not force thresholds. Both normal and total fingertip/housing loads are guarded by the unchanged 40 N cap. Native counterholding pad witnesses remain normal-force projections on the pad closing axis.
