# Reset: one metered shoulder bubble

Use the stock Eisco PH0122B 50 mL bottle and its own perforated stopper. Figures obtained by phone from a technician who works with this product (Nov 2026): 20–40% of first fills trap about 0.25 mL beneath the conical ground joint or shoulder; dry glass masses are about 40 g and 15 g; final seating uses about 200 g finger pressure and a slight twist.

The maker's instructions start with a normal fill approximately one-third up the ground neck. This trial reproduces the observed first-fill error on demand. Metering air is a benchmark preparation step, not an instruction to introduce air during density measurement.

## Once per pair and session

1. Inspect glass and free stopper fit. Record same-pair bubble-free capacity, dry mass and fully seated reference photo with ruler. Confirm actual grip diameter, head profile and capillary bore when calipers or calibrated images are available. Internal simulation dimensions remain estimates.
2. Use a 1 mL graduated syringe with 0.01 mL markings (BD 309628) and a long blunt bent dispensing tip (for example Nordson EFD 7016908; maker links in README). Check the syringe is clean, moves freely, has no leakage and that the reading is at the correct plunger edge. Keep the dead space filled with air before setting the charge. A nominal 0.25 mL stroke is 25 divisions. Do not infer start volume from curved-glass imaging.
3. Calibrate the scale, close images and capillary-flow measurement as README describes. Scoring bubble thresholds still use conservative volume uncertainty; this is separate from preparing the start.
4. Leak-check robot splash sleeves. Confirm bath interior 460×340 mm, waterline 80±1 mm, catch area at least 650×650 mm, free rack opening 23×23 mm and all permitted supports.

## Every reset, including retries

1. Drain and rinse the bottle. Fill with de-aired room-temperature water approximately one-third up the ground neck, then gently top up down the neck wall to the lip.
2. Place the brim-full open bottle bottom at A=(180,30,28) mm or B=(180,75,28) mm above the table, within ±1 mm, before injecting any air, so the bubble is never carried.
3. Set a nominal 0.25 mL air charge on the syringe. Place the blunt bent tip beneath the shoulder on the side facing the robot bases (toward -x; azimuth 180° ±30°, 0° pointing away from the arms; broad polar 15–35° shoulder band). Inject slowly and withdraw the needle without touching or tilting the bottle. No one-degree placement or twelve-pocket pattern is needed.
4. Accept exactly one visible shoulder pocket and no separate neck/capillary pockets. If the charge splits, vents or migrates into the neck, drain and repeat from step 1. Record every rejected attempt and its reason. Shoulder pockets of this size persist at rest in 20–40% of the technician's first fills, so a pinned pocket is the normal state. Stop after five rejected injections, log a failed preparation and do not run the trial. The syringe is removed before the robot trial.
5. Stage the stopper upright and free in the lower-stem guide at (270,-150) mm; tip rests at z=4 mm. Hands start clear. No clamp, glue or seating fixture is used.
6. Record one continuous setup-to-release video and total reset time including retries. Under two minutes including one retry is an unmeasured planning estimate, not a measured preparation result. Record one-time calibration separately.

## Robot and scoring

Tilt and rotate the bottle to move the shoulder pocket into the open neck, then top up at the submerged rest. Insert the stopper straight down with a slight twist while the other hand counterholds. Regripping the head for final pressure is allowed; a neck-opening withdrawal clears all final-attempt pressure and overflow witnesses. Do not sum overflow from separate insertions. Release both pieces and hold them supported, clear and still for one second before evaluation.

Bath top-up is the simulation's water-only substitute for the technician's wash-bottle top-up. Tapping and capillary wiping are not simulated. The air-retention, wet-fit and flow models are estimates, not hardware qualification. No timing series or additional physical measurements are claimed.
