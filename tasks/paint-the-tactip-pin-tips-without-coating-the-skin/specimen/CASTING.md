# Trial skins: mould files, batch casting and enrolment checks

Every trial uses one unpainted skin. No ready-to-order matching unpainted TacTip skin with a published pin layout has been identified
so this folder gives the tooling to cast identical skins in batches. The original casting mould files are generally
shared only through direct academic collaboration (obtained by phone from a technician who works with this product,
Nov 2026), and the original pin layout is not published, so this task supplies its own mould files. The files are generated by `make_mould.py` from the same constants and
the same pin-layout function as task.py, and `check_specimen.py` proves they match (output in `CHECK.txt`).

**Status, stated plainly:** these files have not yet been printed and no skin has been cast from them. Nothing below
is a measured result. The first cast skin from each mould (the "first article") must pass the enrolment checks in
section 4 before any trial uses skins from that mould.

Casting is done ahead of the trials, in batches; it is not part of a trial's reset. Fitting an already enrolled skin is intended to take minutes (not yet timed): fit the
next enrolled skin from stock. Keep at least 8 enrolled skins in stock (4 evaluations of lots A and B) and cast
another batch when the stock falls to 4.

## 1. What the skin is

The specimen has the Open TacTip skin's published size and pin count (a 40 mm hemispherical dome, 532 pins; [Lepora
2021, Table I](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.14455v2)) and is cast in the material named in [Soft Robotics Toolkit, Design](https://softroboticstoolkit.com/tactip/design) (Smooth-On VytaFlex 60, Shore 60A). The pin
layout is this task's own, because the original mould's layout is not published: 15 rings at 0, 9, 15, 21 ... 87
degrees from the apex holding 1, 9, 14, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 43, 47, 50, 52, 54, 56 and 56 pins.

The maker's skin is moulded pins inward and turned inside out over a thumb for painting, so that its pins face
outward. This specimen is cast already in that painting shape, pins outward, so on the rigid former it has exactly
the simulated geometry, with no allowance needed for how a turned skin stretches:

| Feature | Size (mm) |
|---|---|
| Dome | outside radius 20.0, inside radius 19.0 (1.0 wall) |
| Skirt | cylinder, outside radius 20.0, inside 19.0, from the dome's equator up 25.0 to the rim |
| Pins | 532 hexagonal posts, 1.0 across corners (0.87 across flats), standing 2.0 proud of the skin, flat tips |
| Pin tips | on a sphere of radius 22.0 about the dome's centre; the apex pin's tip is 22.0 below the centre, so 47.0 from the rim |
| Index notch | 1.0 wide, 1.0 deep in the rim at azimuth 0 (the only feature not in the simulation; it sits at the rim, against the former's shank) |
| Volume, mass | 6,143 mm3, so 6.39 g at VytaFlex 60's 1.04 g/cm3 (computed from the mesh, not weighed) |

`pins.csv` lists every pin (id, ring, angle from the apex, azimuth from the notch, root and tip points in mm, frame:
origin at the dome's centre, +z from the apex toward the rim). `pin_atlas.svg` is the numbered map the setter and
the scorer count against. The skin is a trial specimen, cast pins outward in its painting shape; this task does not test bonding it to a sensor body.

The geometry constants still match task.py, which `check_specimen.py` confirms. Pin height about 2 mm, tip
diameter 1 mm, wall about 1 mm and painting press about 0.4 N were obtained by phone from a technician who works
with this product (Nov 2026). The task-designed hexagonal cross section, ring layout and VytaFlex replica
have not been mechanically calibrated.

## 2. The files

| File | Print | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `master.stl` | once, resin (MSLA/SLA, Anycubic High Clear Resin 405 nm; qualify the actual print by the coupon in A2, 0.05 mm layers or finer; 59 cm3 solid) | the skin's outside with all 532 pins, a locating plug (radius 19.0) running 9 mm above the rim (8 mm forms the mould's socket, 1 mm sits in the base's recess), and four 1.2 mm vent ribs on the plug from the rim |
| `box_base.stl`, `box_tube.stl` | one base, four tubes for four simultaneous moulds; any printer (PLA is fine) | the pouring box for the silicone mould: the master's plug end sits in the base's 1 mm recess (0.15 mm clearance) and the tube (66 mm inside) stands in the base's ring (0.2 mm clearance); the tube is also the sleeve that holds the slit silicone mould closed for each skin pour |
| `core.stl` | one per silicone mould, same resin (61 cm3 solid; or PLA at 0.1 mm layers, sanded and sealed) | the skin's inside: the painting former's radius-19 dome and body, a 65.6 mm flange that seats on the mould's face inside the tube (so the 1 mm wall is set by the parts, not by hand), a funnel and 3 mm sprue down the axis to the apex, and a groove under the flange with four holes that let the rim vents breathe |
| `silicone_mould_reference.stl`, `skin_reference.stl` | do not print | what the silicone mould and the cast skin become; for checking (both carry 532 pin holes or pins on task.py's tip points) |
| `mould_section.svg` | - | section on the axis of the mould, core and skin as poured |

Orient the master with its plug's flat end face (38 mm across) on the build plate, rotating the CAD model so
the dome and apex rise away from the plate. Inspect the sliced layers for unsupported pin features; do not place
support contacts on the skin-forming dome or pins. This print orientation has not been tested. A print service can quote from the STL files and the
volumes above.
Check the printed mating clearances before casting: the plug has 0.15 mm radial clearance in the base recess;
the tube has 0.2 mm radial clearance in the base ring; the core flange has 0.2 mm radial clearance inside the tube.
These are CAD clearances, not a guarantee of printer accuracy. Dry-fit the parts; remove burrs or lightly sand only
the base recess/ring, tube mating surfaces and core flange's outer edge until they seat without force. Do not sand
the master's pins or skin surface, the core's skin-forming dome/body, or the flange's seating face; reprint if those
surfaces are out of size. Recheck the fits after sealing.
Rebuild everything with `python make_mould.py` (numpy and manifold3d); `MANIFEST.sha256` holds the hashes.

Silicone, urethane and release subtotal USD 76.65; with black colorant USD 93.16. With the candidate resin USD 133.15. These are partial materials only; regional availability/shipping and printing are unquoted. The resin is a named candidate, not a tested compatible print. Each silicone mould takes 164 cm3 (194 g), so one
Mold Star 15 SLOW trial unit (907 g) makes four moulds. Each skin takes 6.4 g plus its sprue, vents and groove (about 7.4 g, 7.1 mL); mixing about 45 g of
VytaFlex 60 for a batch of four skins, one trial unit (907 g) gives about 20 batches, about 80 skins (about
USD 0.33 of urethane per skin before rejects; actual acceptable yield is unmeasured). A resin printer or a print service is needed once for the master and cores
(print services quote after upload; no price is given here). A vacuum chamber for degassing, or a pressure pot,
is recommended (not priced here); section 4 says when it becomes necessary. Wear vinyl gloves (latex stops platinum silicone from
curing, per the Mold Star 15 SLOW page); work in a ventilated area and follow each product's safety data sheet.

**A. Tooling (once)**
1. Print the master and the cores. Wash in isopropyl alcohol, then soap and water, dry, and post-cure under UV for
   at least 6 hours, turning the parts (Smooth-On FAQ 210). Check under a 10x loupe that all 532 pins are present,
   whole and 2 mm long against `pin_atlas.svg`; reprint if any is missing.
2. Seal the master with a light mist of clear acrylic gloss and let it dry; do not fill the corners of the pins.
   Apply Ease Release 200 and let it dry. Smooth-On warns that some newly cast materials stop this silicone curing
   and asks for printed models to be fully UV-cured and sealed, so first pour a spoonful of
   mixed Mold Star 15 SLOW on a sealed scrap print of the same resin; continue only if it cures fully in 4 hours.
3. Set the master's plug end in the base's recess, dome up. Tack the accessible plug-to-base edge with a small bead
   of hot glue, keeping glue out of the silicone cavity. Stand the tube in the base's ring and seal its outside joint
   with hot glue (no sulfur-based modelling clay). Keep both glue joints accessible for cutting in A5.
4. Mix 210 g of Mold Star 15 SLOW (105 g A + 105 g B), degas it if a vacuum chamber is at hand, and pour it in a thin
   stream down the tube's wall (not onto the pins) until it stands 10 mm over the apex pin (3 mm below the tube's top).
   Cure 4 hours at 23 C.
5. Cut both hot-glue joints from A3: the tube-to-base seal and the master plug-to-base tack. Separate the base
   without levering on the dome or pins, lift the tube away and push the mould out. Slit one side of the mould with
   a zig-zag cut, through the full silicone wall from the outer face into the master cavity, continuously from the
   socket opening along the skirt and dome to just beyond the apex pin cavity. The cut depth follows the cavity;
   a shallow surface score will not release the pins. Avoid cutting the master. The zig-zag keys the cut faces back
   together. Open the slit and peel the silicone away to lift the master out without dragging the pins through the
   socket (whose radius is 3 mm under the pin tips'). The tube holds the slit closed for every pour.
6. Repeat 4-5 for up to four moulds (or pour them in parallel from four boxes).

**B. Skins (each batch: one skin per mould, 16 hours)**
1. Apply Ease Release 200 in each mould and on each core; let it dry 30 minutes (the VytaFlex 60 page's drying
   time for a release coat).
2. Put each mould in its tube, apex down, socket up, the cut closed. Push a core into the socket until its flange
   sits flat on the mould's face all round, inside the tube.
3. At 23 C and below 50% relative humidity, pre-mix Part B thoroughly. Measure about 45 g of VytaFlex 60
   (1A:1B), add black SO-Strong to Part B per its datasheet (0.01-3% of total weight; record the amount), then combine A/B and mix thoroughly for at least 3 minutes, scraping sides and
   bottom (manufacturer instructions). Degas if a vacuum chamber is at hand. Draw about 8 mL into a 10 mL syringe and inject it slowly through
   each core's funnel into the 3 mm sprue. The urethane enters at the apex and rises through the dome to the rim,
   pushing air up out of the pin holes and through the vents; stop when it shows in the flange's groove at all four
   holes. Top up the sprue after 5 minutes.
4. Cure at least 16 hours at 23 C. Pull each core out by its flange (twist a little to break the release), then take
   the mould out of the tube. Reopen the slit made in A5 and peel the silicone away from the skin and each pin;
   do not pull the pinned dome through the narrower socket with the slit closed. Cut the sprue's stub flush on the
   inside of the apex and the four vent stubs flush at the skin's rim.
5. Leave each skin at room temperature for 7 days before enrolment and use (a benchmark choice, so that all skins are
   tested at the same state of cure; the 16 hours above is only the demould time). Enrol each skin (section 4),
   label it with its batch and number, and store it flat in a bag out of the light.

Nominal capacity is four castings per 16-hour cure, before rejects; one evaluation (lots A and B) uses two accepted skins. No measured scrap allowance or acceptable yield is available.

## 4. Enrolment checks (every skin, before use)

These are this benchmark's acceptance limits, not the maker's. Tools: 10x loupe; camera with a macro lens and a
0.5 mm-division scale in the frame; digital calipers reading 0.01 mm; a scale reading 0.01 g; the painting former; a
100 g mass on a thread.

| Check | Accept | How |
|---|---|---|
| Pin count | 532: per ring 1, 9, 14, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 43, 47, 50, 52, 54, 56, 56 | count ring by ring under the loupe against `pin_atlas.svg`, starting at the rim notch |
| Pin defects | none missing, short, torn, fused to a neighbour, bent over or with a bubble in the tip; every tip flat | loupe, all round |
| Pin size | 1.0 +-0.1 mm across corners; 2.0 +-0.15 mm proud of the skin | macro photo with the scale: the apex pin, and 5 pins each of the 45 and 87 degree rings, end-on (across corners) and in profile (length) |
| Tip sphere | 43.9 +-0.4 mm across opposite tips of the 87 degree ring (2 x 22 x sin 87 = 43.94), measured on the former | calipers, at three azimuths |
| Dome and skirt | 40.0 +-0.3 mm across the skirt; 47.0 +-0.5 mm from the apex pin's tip to the rim; wall 1.0 +-0.15 mm at the rim | calipers, on the former (skirt, height) and at the rim at four points (wall) |
| Mass | 6.39 +-0.4 g after trimming | scale |
| Surface | no white or grey specks, tears or bubbles on the skin between the pins (they would read as paint) | loupe |
| Fit | slides onto the painting former without folds, the skin's rim within 1 mm of the former's shank all round, and does not turn on the former when a 100 g mass hangs from a thread wound once round the skirt (about 0.02 N m) | by hand, former held handle up |

A skin that fails any check is not used. Record each skin's checks with its label. The first article from each new
mould is checked in full and photographed; if it fails, the cause (unfilled pins, bubbles, a wall off-centre) is
fixed before the batch is used. Whether 1 mm urethane pins fill completely in this mould without vacuum has not been
tested: that is the first article's main check. If its pins are not all filled, degas every urethane batch in a vacuum
chamber, and if pins still fail, cure the filled moulds in a pressure pot; re-check a new first article after any
change.
