# Lower the Yaris parking brake at its unmodified grip

Status: version 0.19.4 has a full scripted 480 Hz DEMO score of 1.0 in both lots on the frozen runtime. Both finished at 39.95 seconds, 46.817 mm clear for 1.0354 seconds. Full raw audits show 56.01 N maximum finger resultant and no advancing-stop, cap or integrity failure. The 960 Hz comparison also completed 1.0/1.0. The single Dream-1 run on the same frozen source and instruction scored 0 in both lots: it reached the 2400-step limit without releasing the lever. Physical OEM fit, received material certificate, force-curve enrollment, proof loads and thermal qualification remain required; no hardware score is claimed.

Drivers release a mechanical parking brake before driving. A full-size approximation of the Toyota Yaris lever and bracket sits between seat and console envelopes. A parallel gripper with passive under-grip shoes supports the original grip while the second presses the original button. The holding radius is 228 mm, inside the 170-272 mm stock grip; no extension or powered helper is attached. A parallel-jaw gripper has no opposable thumb; the second arm presses the button where a thumb does, on the unmodified grip and button, with no added moment arm. The holding hand bears the full measured release load inside the 220 mm seat gap and 80 mm console slot. Its passive 27 × 22 × 4 mm steel feet act as under-grip fingertip pads against the 35 mm grip; they attach to the robot, not the lever.

## Load, mechanism and score

Figures obtained by phone from a technician who works with this product (Nov 2026), measured on the car, a 2018 Toyota Yaris XP130: perpendicular release pull 54 N at four clicks and 210 mm from pivot, approximately proportional to clicks; normal application five clicks, about five degrees per click. Slack return pull 3.9 N; button force 7.4 N through 8 mm. These replace the former application-force estimate and the rejected adapted-control ratio. fixture/measurement.md gives the on-car procedure for repeating these readings.

The reported lower cable-equivalent moment is `0.210 * max(3.9, 2.7 * angle_deg)` N m: 14.175 N m at the normal 25 degree start. The runtime uses the larger of that moment and a declared progressive bench reference, then adds conservative model gravity. Each exact TOHATSU spring has a rate of 54.48 N/mm. The reference effective original-rod radius of 35 mm and total preload of 1 mm are design estimates, not Yaris measurements. With `x = 0.001 + 0.035*sin(theta)`, reference rod force is `27240*(x + max(0, x-0.016))` N and torque is `0.035*cos(theta)*force`. An 8 mm front-spring compression stop changes the two-series rate from 27.24 to 54.48 N/mm. A 22.1 mm total compression stop protects both springs; operating compression must remain at most 22.0 mm. This raises intermediate-angle resistance where the single-rate design could miss the reported curve. The model never falls below the reported load. Complete physical enrollment must fit both the reported lower bound and the declared model upper envelope. A nominal spring rate does not establish the OEM transfer. Zero clicks at the down stop is assumed; other click/angle targets are scaled values, not additional readings.

The button has a constant passive return load of 7.5962 N (reported 7.4 N plus the worst-case gravity of its 20 g slider), stiffness zero and damping 2 N s/m. This envelopes the reported force throughout all 8 mm of inward motion without assuming an unreported spring curve. Full-stroke potential is 0.0607696 J before gravity. Release still requires the full 8 mm stroke. The original solid boss collider was replaced by annular walls around the estimated 15 mm bore, retaining the outer diameter and original button. A loaded pawl blocks the button at 1 mm until a physical 1 degree lift. Below 4.5 mm button return, the pawl catches the lower 5 degree tooth. Pawl/jam are limit proxies, with no in-trial pose or velocity writes. Stops do not supply lifting work. Episode reset installs the 1 mm loaded stop and full passive loads immediately after standard zero-joint reset and before any physics; this initialization is separate from in-trial stop changes.

The score uses no continuous force, signed holding moment or assigned hand roles. Final supported angle ≤20 degrees gives 0.25; ≤16 gives 0.5; ≤8 gives 0.75; the bottom stop within 0.5 degree gives 1.0; otherwise zero. All credit requires release, a supported lower tooth/bottom stop, returned button and both complete hands at least 30 mm clear of the lever and button for one second. An 8 degree fall within 0.1 second permanently gives zero.

For a real trial, use synchronized 30 fps side/oblique video, the fixed protractor, a flush/out witness mark on the original button and the 30 mm clearance reference. Record the lift off the tooth, button visibly held in, a hand visibly on the grip throughout descent, final supported angle, button visibly out, and both hands 30 mm clear of the lever and button for at least 30 frames. A drop of 8 degrees in three frames is a failure. The witness mark changes no button geometry or resistance.

Simulation integrity checks additionally require button return ≤1.1 mm, lever speed <0.5 degree/s, button speed <2 mm/s, no more than 0.25 degree cumulative unheld descent, and finite observations with no physics sampling gap >5 ms. Out-of-travel joints or displaced mount/link geometry invalidate the episode. These fine numerical checks use simulator observations; they are not measurements claimed from 30 fps video. Conservative surface sampling subtracts 5 mm before the 30 mm lever-and-button clearance test.

## Scene and embodiment

Pivot (300,-420,68) mm above tabletop; world-x hinge, yaw zero. Lever end radius 297 mm; grip diameter 35 mm spanning 170–272 mm. Floor 440x580x18 mm at local (0,150,-59) mm. Seats 89x560x140 mm at (+/-154.5,150,20) mm leave 220 mm. Console cheeks 38x420x38 mm at (+/-59,135,-31) mm leave 80 mm; rear 80x30x38 mm at (0,-60,-31) mm. These are stated estimates, not OEM cabin dimensional data. The enclosed rod tunnel is 116×180×116 mm at local (0,-168,-10). Rear load box 116×240×180 at (0,-360,-20) starts 10 mm beyond the bench rear edge and extends below it; shelf 220×260×18 at (0,-360,-119). Two edge-on 400×25×6.35 steel rails, four 85 mm hangers, bench feet and shelf cross-supports are collidable. The shelf/bolt assembly uses a conservative 220×260×60 mm collision envelope at local (0,-360,-115), and the complete bench stud/foot/underside spreaders use 80×100×120 mm envelopes at (±90,-180,-98); the physical shelf remains 18 mm thick. The rear support uses 118 mm studs through the 60 mm bench; the complete OEM clamp/key mount uses ten 158 mm studs and explicit below-table bounds. The fixed parts use their actual front mounting datum on the bench and explicitly offset their full rear geometry; the whole guard is not claimed to fit on the tabletop. Complete positions in dimensions.json. Angle scale at local x=100 mm. Cosmetic A/B lots move a loose card only; they permit replay and are not generalization evidence.

YAM arms with two passive 50 g holding-finger shoes retain 27 N m shoulder, 7 N m wrist and 50 N per-finger drive limits. The right holding fingers and stock grip form the demonstrated zero-friction pair; the pressing hand retains the supplied contact materials. The release load goes through the bearing shoes. Motor identities and stock gains follow the [maker YAM configuration](fixture/parameters.md); actuator maxima follow the supplied embodiment. Native integrity rejects raised, nonfinite or missing caps every physics step. The PhysX performance-envelope API enforces the same limits on the sum of implicit PD and external gravity feed-forward. [NVIDIA API documentation](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/omni_physics/107.3/dev_guide/rigid_bodies_articulations/articulations.html). Both arms and the brake use 128 position/8 velocity solver iterations; physical gains, caps and masses are unchanged.

The demo uses the default HB_PULL_GAIN, HB_PULL_BIAS, HB_PRESS_N, HB_LIFT_DEG and HB_PRESS_TILT, without tuning overrides. It computes fixed position targets from geometry, declared loads and stock gains; it reads no observations, lot ID or scorer state. Lifting and button compression require physical contact against the stated resistance; passive cable load drives lowering, which must be controlled by grip contact. During button-held descent, contact must be on the 170–272 mm grip; touching the button boss does not count. The holding hand relaxes to a 38 mm commanded gap, lowers its feet 20 mm, and leans forward while sliding to radius 320 mm with fixed base yaw. It raises the feet beyond the button before opening above the console.

## Arm load and validation

The frozen 480 Hz DEMO reconstructs holding shoulder/elbow demand peaks 24.852/22.071 N m and 9-second means 22.043/19.430 N m during 8.5–17.5 seconds. Shoulder peak is about 92% of the 27 N m cap. All sampled arm requests remain below the stock caps. These post-step PD-plus-gravity estimates are not motor-sensor measurements. The [DAMIAO specification](https://www.damiaokeji.com/index.php?c=show&id=100) gives 9 N m rated and 27 N m peak, with no certified nine-second peak-duration allowance. Real enrollment must qualify the complete current/temperature duty cycle and holding gripper load path; no side-load rating follows from the 50 N closing-drive cap. Native combined caps cover implicit PD plus external gravity. A simulation score is not thermal or physical proof.
